How the engine works

What the model looks at for each player, and how that becomes a projection.

  • Recent form, weighted. His last game, his last 3 and last 5, and a weighted average that cares more about lately — plus the direction he's trending.
  • Opportunity. Snap share, target and carry shares, air yards, red-zone work. Role is stickier than raw points, so the model trusts role more.
  • The matchup. How many points the opponent has been giving up to his position lately.
  • The game environment. The Vegas spread, the game total, and each team's implied points — high implied totals mean more scoring to share around. Kickers and defenses are projected mostly from these lines directly.
  • Availability and context. Injury and practice reports, depth-chart rank, teammates out at his position, rest days, home or away, and last season's scoring rate for players with thin history.
  • A separate model per position. Quarterbacks, running backs, receivers, and tight ends each get their own model, retrained on every week in the database before this one — using only information that existed before that week's games. No peeking at results.
  • An honest range, not just a number. Every projection is a middle (the headline), a bad-week end, and a great-week end. Yahoo's number sits next to ours so the difference is always visible.
  • Graded in public. The scores above come from replaying history week by week and comparing predictions with what actually happened. Live predictions currently use the model's raw range; the calibrated range from the backtest is the next upgrade.
