AUSPICIA Control Center

AUSPICIA (aw-SPEE-chee-uh) fantasy football analytics engine

is designed to help manage team rosters by projecting weekly fantasy points per player. This page explains the architecture and mechanisms behind the machine.

  • The engine — backstage. Its only job is to make the projections better: ingest data, build features, train, predict, evaluate, diagnose.
  • The dashboard — the product. It plugs in the latest model's weekly numbers and shows verdicts, plainly. It does not explain the engine.
  • The Control Center — this page. The manual for the engine: how each feature works, where the project stands, and how the model is actually doing.

How it all fits together

One contract worth remembering: Yahoo is not training fuel. It supplies the league's rosters (who to predict for) and Yahoo's own projections (the rival the scorecard grades against). Training data is NFLverse history, scored under this league's exact rules.

How the engine works

The pipeline, step by step. Every prediction is made using only information that existed before that week's games — no peeking at results.

  • 1 Ingest. NFLverse play-by-play, player stats, schedules, and Vegas lines are loaded into a local store covering 2018 onward. Every row carries the moment it became known, so history can be replayed honestly.
  • 2 Features. Raw facts become per-player-week signals: recent form (last game, last 3, last 5, a recency-weighted average, trend direction), opportunity (snap share, target and carry shares, air yards, red-zone work), matchup strength, and the game environment (spread, total, implied team points).
  • 3 Train. Each skill position gets its own LightGBM model — one for QB, one for RB, one for WR, one for TE — because each position scores in its own way and is driven by different signals. Each model is trained on past seasons, with every past game scored under this league's exact rules (non-PPR, 6-point passing TDs, the 40+ yard TD bonus), and it projects only its own position's players for the coming week. Kickers and defenses use a simpler regression built mostly on the Vegas lines.
  • 4 Predict. Each player gets a distribution, not a single number: a mean (expected points — the lineup is picked on this), a median (the typical week, the headline on his card), and a 10th–90th range (bad week to great week). Win probability is arithmetic on the two teams' totals.
  • 5 Evaluate. Two kinds of grading. The backtest replays 2022–2025 week by week — 72 rounds, training only on the past each time — and produces the benchmarks below. The weekly scorecard freezes live predictions before kickoff and grades them against actuals and Yahoo once the week is final.

Known gaps, stated plainly: live weekly predictions use the model's raw ranges — the conformal calibration that holds up in the backtest is not applied live yet (that's M7 work). Win probability assumes a fixed spread of outcomes (standard deviation 25) that has not been fitted to results yet.

Promotion gates

Promoted: No Model lgbm_skill_v1 · fails on bias only · the fix is the model, never the ruler

What is being promoted is the model itself — from "in use" to "proven." It is a title, not a switch. The dashboard already runs this model's projections: every number on the Team page this week is the machine's, not a placeholder and not Yahoo's. Promotion doesn't decide whether the model gets used. It decides whether it has earned the claim "proven" — the way a pilot flies the plane long before the checkride, and passing the checkride is what makes it official. This model works without the title because it hasn't passed yet, and this page says so in the open. Calling it proven first and checking later would be grading our own homework.

  • 1 · Accuracy beats the legacy baseline (q50 MAE, at least 3 of 4 seasons)PASS
  • 2 · No position left behind vs the baselinePASS
  • 3 · Mean bias within ±0.3 points, every position, every seasonFAIL

  • 5 · Lineup points gained vs the baseline is not negativePASS

Gate 3's seven failures — all from the 2022–2025 backtest, mean head:

  • QB 2022 · +0.66
  • WR 2022 · +0.40
  • QB 2023 · +0.70
  • K 2024 · −0.57
  • QB 2025 · +0.72
  • K 2025 · −0.55
  • DST 2025 · −1.51

The diagnosis so far: the quarterback overprediction is real but not a constant offset (QB 2024 sits at −0.05), and the K/DST 2025 failures are recent and tail-driven. The next engine step is a pre-registered, mechanism-level fix — written down as DESIGN §22 before it runs. One loose end is recorded openly first: the bias figures reproduce across reruns to about a thousandth of a point, but one rerun produced a different report digest and the cause is not yet pinned down. No fix gets built on an unexplained digest.

Benchmarks — the 2022–2025 backtest

How the model was graded: history was replayed week by week — 72 weeks across 2022–2025. For each week, a fresh model was trained using only games played before that week, then asked to project that week sight unseen. Accuracy is MAE on the median projection: on average, how many points the headline number missed what each player actually scored by. The baseline is the older, simpler projector this model replaced — a fixed blend of a player's own recent scoring averages, with no learning and no matchup or Vegas inputs.

Definitions

Every label the dashboard uses, in plain language. This is their permanent home; the dashboard keeps only the numbers.

Mean
Expected points: his average outcome across many similar weeks. The recommended lineup is the one that maximizes total mean.
Median
The typical week: half his outcomes land above it, half below. It is the big number on his row — but the lineup is picked on the mean, not the median.
10th–90th range
The model's 10th–90th percentile range: a bad week lands near the low end, a great week near the high end. Live ranges are the model's raw quantiles, not yet calibrated.
Boom/bust
How wide his range is versus the same position this week, by percentile of the 10th–90th width: Steady under 34, Balanced 34–66, Boom/bust 67 and over. Wide means more swing week to week — not more points.
Opp rank
Toughness of the opposing defense against his position, by fantasy points allowed per game this season in this league's scoring. 1 = toughest (allows the fewest).
Vegas implied
Points Vegas implies his NFL team will score, from the spread and the game total. Higher team totals usually mean more scoring to go around.
Practice
His latest practice report this week: FP full, LP limited, DNP did not practice. Blank means he was not on the injury report — not a guarantee he plays.
Snap share
Share of his team's offensive snaps in his most recent game, and how it moved since the first week shown.
Last 6 weeks
His actual fantasy points in each of the last six weeks he played, oldest to newest.
Start · Monitor · Sit
Start = in the recommended lineup. Monitor = a close call, flagged because a legal bench replacement is within the close-call margin. Sit = not in the recommended lineup.
Projected total
The sum of the recommended starters' mean projections.
Win probability
Chance of outscoring the opponent, computed from the two projected totals assuming a typical spread of outcomes around them.
Yahoo chip
Our median minus Yahoo's projection for him. Green = we are higher, red = Yahoo is higher.
Bias
The model's systematic lean: on average, how far its projections sit above or below what players actually score, sign included. Positive bias means it habitually over-projects (QB 2025 at +0.72 = quarterbacks penciled in for about seven-tenths of a point too many, week after week); negative means it under-projects. Where MAE measures scatter in both directions, bias measures drift in one — a model can be close on average and still lean. Gate 3 is the bias gate: no position, in any season, may lean more than ±0.3.
MAE
Mean absolute error — on average, how many points a projection missed by, counting over- and under-projections equally. A q50 MAE of 3.65 means the median projection typically landed within about 3.65 points of what the player actually scored. Lower is better; MAE is the yardstick behind the accuracy gate and the benchmark table.
LightGBM
The machine-learning library the engine's models are built with. It grows many small decision trees in sequence — each new tree is trained to correct the mistakes the trees before it made, a technique called gradient boosting — until their combined judgment settles. Here, one LightGBM model per skill position learns from thousands of past player-weeks how form, opportunity, matchup, and game environment turned into fantasy points under this league's scoring. This is the ML in the machine; kickers and defenses are the exception, projected by a simpler Vegas-based regression instead.
Candidate model
A newly built version of the projection model — carrying a fix, a change, or a retrain — that has not earned the job yet. It changes nothing the dashboard shows. First it is run through the full evaluation and the five promotion gates; only if it passes all five does it replace the model in use (see Promoted).
Promotion gate
One of five pass/fail checks the model must clear to earn the title "proven": accuracy beats the baseline; no position left behind; bias within ±0.3 points at every position; interval coverage inside its band; and lineup points gained not negative. All five must pass — failing one gate is enough to stay unpromoted.
Promoted
The model's title, not its job. A promoted model has passed all five gates and earned the claim "proven." The dashboard runs the current model's numbers whether it holds the title or not: promotion changes what we claim, not what we use.
DESIGN §20
The promotion gates as written in the project's design document: the five pass/fail checks a candidate model must clear to earn the title "proven" — accuracy, no position left behind, bias, coverage, and lineup value — listed in full in the gates card on this page. When this page says the model fails §20, it fails those five checks, nothing vaguer.
DESIGN §22
The design document's pre-registration rule for model fixes: before a mechanism-level change is allowed to run, its hypothesis and what would count as success are written down — so a result cannot be explained away afterward, and a failed idea stays failed on paper. The bias fix will be the first entry under it.
